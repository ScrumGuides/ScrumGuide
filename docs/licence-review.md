# Licence review — the historical Scrum Guides

This file is the evidence for a publishing decision. Specification §9.1 makes the licence on
each individual edition a blocking condition: the November 2020 Scrum Guide is offered under
the Creative Commons Attribution ShareAlike licence, and the earlier editions were unverified.
The 2020 terms were not assumed to run backwards. Each edition below was checked against the
copyright and licence notice printed **in that edition's own document**.

Reviewed 19 November 2026.

## Per-edition findings

### 2010.2 — February 2010 — do not publish

Title page: "February 2010 / Scrum: Developed and sustained by Ken Schwaber and Jeff
Sutherland". The page footer, on every page, reads in full:

> © 2008-2010 Ken Schwaber and Jeff Sutherland, All Rights Reserved

That is the only copyright or licence statement in the document. There is no Creative Commons
grant, no permission to copy, redistribute or adapt, and no end note qualifying the reservation.
"All Rights Reserved" is the opposite of a re-hosting permission, so the body is not published.
The edition page keeps its placeholder, names the authors and links the canonical source.

The publishers do not serve this document at all today, so there is also no later re-publication
of it under other terms to rely on.

### 2011.10 — October 2011 — do not publish

Title page: "The Scrum Guide / The Definitive Guide to Scrum: The Rules of the Game / October
2011 / Developed and sustained by Ken Schwaber and Jeff Sutherland". The page footer, on every
page, reads in full:

> © 1991-2011 Ken Schwaber and Jeff Sutherland, All Rights Reserved

Again the only statement in the document, and again no Creative Commons grant. The document's
Conclusion says "Scrum is free and offered in this guide", which is a statement about Scrum the
framework being free to use, not a copyright licence for the text of the guide — the footer on
the same page reserves all rights. Reading that sentence as a licence would contradict the
notice printed beside it. The body is not published.

### 2013.7 — July 2013 — publish

Title page: "The Scrum Guide™ / The Definitive Guide to Scrum: The Rules of the Game / July
2013 / Developed and sustained by Ken Schwaber and Jeff Sutherland". The file the publishers
serve today for this edition carries, on every page:

> ©2014 Scrum.Org and ScrumInc. Offered for license under the Attribution Share-Alike license
> of Creative Commons, accessible at http://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/legalcode
> and also described in summary form at http://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/. By
> utilizing this Scrum Guide you acknowledge and agree that you have read and agree to be
> bound by the terms of the Attribution Share-Alike license of Creative Commons.

**This edition changed its notice, and that is worth stating plainly.** The same URL, captured
on 30 October 2014, served a document whose footer read "© 1991-2014 Ken Schwaber and Jeff
Sutherland, All Rights Reserved" — no Creative Commons grant. The Creative Commons notice above
is therefore a later re-licensing of the July 2013 text by its rightsholders, not the notice the
edition first appeared with.

Conclusion: publish. The decision rests on the document the rightsholders publish for this
edition now, which grants CC BY-SA 4.0 over this text. A rightsholder may license their own work
at any time, and this is the current, live statement of terms from the publishers themselves.
The page reproduces the current file's text and its ©2014 notice together, so a reader sees the
notice that goes with the text they are reading. If this reading is ever disputed, the fix is to
revert this edition to a placeholder; nothing else on the site depends on it.

## Share-alike

CC BY-SA 4.0 requires that an adapted version be licensed on the same terms. The four published
pages are reproductions, not adaptations: the text is unaltered and each page carries its
document's own copyright notice, the authors' names and a link to the canonical source. Nothing
on those pages is offered under terms more restrictive than CC BY-SA 4.0.

## How the text was transcribed

Each published edition was converted from the publishers' own file. Nothing was summarised,
modernised, corrected, reordered or added to the guide text.

- **2013.7, 2016.7, 2017.11** — from the PDF. Headings, paragraph boundaries, list structure and
  inline emphasis were taken from the document's own typesetting (heading styles at 24pt, 21pt
  and 18pt; body at 17pt), and the resulting markdown was then compared character for character
  against an independent text extraction of the same PDF. The only differences are list markers,
  which markdown writes as syntax, and hyphens restored in `self-organizing`, `time-box`,
  `burn-downs` and `one-month`, which the document breaks across lines and the extractor drops.
- **2020.11** — from the HTML edition the publishers serve at scrumguides.org, which already
  carries the document's heading hierarchy, and verified the same way against the PDF.

Three transcription decisions, each applying to every edition:

1. **Heading depth, not point size.** Each document's top-level sections become `##`, their
   subsections `###`, and sub-subsections `####`. In 2013, 2016 and 2017 the authors styled some
   second-level sections (Transparency, Inspection, Adaptation) with the third-level heading
   style, skipping a level. Those are rendered at their nesting depth rather than their point
   size, so the table of contents and the anchor links have no gap in them. The publishers' own
   2020 HTML does the same thing with the same headings.
2. **The title block is not repeated in the body.** Each document opens with its title,
   subtitle, date and byline. The title and date are already in the page's front matter, so the
   body opens with the subtitle, date and byline in a short notice, then the first section.
3. **Two things in the publishers' files are not guide text and are not carried.** The running
   page footer, which repeats the copyright notice on every page — that notice appears once, at
   the foot of the body, where the document also prints it in full. And, on the 2020 page, the
   line scrumguides.org adds to its HTML saying that the HTML is a port of the PDF, plus the
   "Ken Schwaber & Jeff Sutherland November 2020" signature that the PDF prints after the
   Purpose section and the publishers' own HTML omits.

## Two things this review turned up, for someone else to decide

1. **A July 2011 edition is missing from the site.** The Scrum Guide Revision History, as the
   publishers served it in November 2011, lists a **July 2011 release** between February 2010
   and October 2011, and describes it as "a significant update with many changes" — release
   planning, release burndown and the pigs-and-chickens metaphor removed, commitment replaced
   by forecast, "Team" renamed "Development Team", Product Backlog ordered rather than
   prioritised. October 2011 is then called "a minor release with mostly cosmetic changes". The
   site's edition set (specification §5.3) goes 2010.2 → 2011.10 and omits 2011.7. That is an
   editorial decision to take knowingly, not a gap to fill silently. Its licence would need the
   same check, and the February 2010 and October 2011 findings above suggest the answer would be
   the same.
2. **A November 2009 document exists.** scrum.org served it as `Scrum Guide.pdf` until some time
   after February 2010, and it predates what the site calls the first edition. It carries
   "© 2008-2009 Ken Schwaber and Jeff Sutherland, All Rights Reserved". Whether the lineage
   starts in November 2009 or February 2010 is a question for the site, not for this review.

## When to revisit

- If a publisher re-publishes the February 2010 or October 2011 edition under Creative Commons,
  that edition can be published here. Nothing short of a licence grant from the rightsholders
  changes those two findings.
- If the file scrumguides.org serves for the July 2013 edition changes its notice again, re-read
  this file's §2013.7 before changing that page.
