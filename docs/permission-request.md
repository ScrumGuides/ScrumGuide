# Permission requests for the five pre-Creative-Commons editions

Two emails. Send, and the moment either comes back yes, the text goes up the same day — the
documents are already fetched, verified and run through the same pipeline as the eight that
are live.

What is being asked for, precisely: permission to reproduce the full text of these editions on
a non-commercial archive site, unaltered, with the edition's own copyright notice on the page,
the authors named, and the canonical source linked.

---

## 1. To scrumguides.org — the November 2009, February 2010, July 2011 and October 2011 Scrum Guides

**To:** support@scrumguides.org
**Subject:** Permission to re-host the pre-2013 Scrum Guide editions on an archive site

Ken, Jeff,

I run a site that presents every published version of the Scrum Guide as one lineage, so a
reader can move between editions and see how the definition of Scrum changed. It is
non-commercial and it is not presented as an official Scrum.org or scrumguides.org property.

The 2013, 2016, 2017 and 2020 editions are offered under CC BY-SA 4.0, so those four carry
their full text, unaltered, each with its own copyright notice on the page and scrumguides.org
linked as the canonical source.

The four editions before them — November 2009, February 2010, July 2011 and October 2011 —
carry "All Rights Reserved" and no licence, so I have not reproduced them. Those pages
currently describe the edition and link the archived file instead.

May I have permission to reproduce the full text of those four editions on the same terms as
the CC ones: unaltered, with each edition's own copyright notice reproduced on the page, you
both named as the authors, scrumguides.org linked as the canonical source, and no claim of
authorship or endorsement?

If it is easier, applying CC BY-SA 4.0 to those four editions retroactively — as was done for
the July 2013 edition, whose current file carries a 2014 Creative Commons notice where the 2014
file carried "All Rights Reserved" — would settle it for everyone, not just for me.

Happy to send you the URLs to review first.

Martin Hinshelwood

I present every published version of the Nexus Guide as one lineage on a non-commercial

May I have permission to reproduce the 2015 edition in full on the same terms as the 2018 and

source?

Alternatively, extending the CC BY-SA 4.0 licence that the 2018 and 2021 editions already carry

Martin Hinshelwood

## Why this is worth two emails rather than a workaround

A Professional Scrum Trainer agreement is a licence to teach, not an assignment of copyright,
so it does not carry the right to republish these particular documents — the 2009 to 2011
Scrum Guides are Schwaber and Sutherland's, and the 2015 Nexus Guide is Scrum.org's. But being
a PST is exactly why these two emails are likely to be short conversations rather than long
ones: you have the internal route at Scrum.org, and the precedent already exists in the July
2013 Scrum Guide, which the rightsholders re-licensed to CC BY-SA after first publishing it
all-rights-reserved.
