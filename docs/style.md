# Style

Every sentence proposed for a guide or a change note is tested against these rules before
it is shown. A sentence that fails is fixed before it is shown.

## Sentences

- State one claim per sentence, in the present tense: subject, verb, complement.
- Keep a sentence between 3 and 35 words. Aim for about 15.
- Keep a paragraph to two to five sentences.
- Put a subordinate clause after the main clause, and keep it short.
- Keep the subject within four words of its verb.
- Use the passive only when the actor is deliberately unnamed. Never stack passives.
- Use a semicolon only to end a list item.
- Use no em-dash asides.
- Repeat the noun when a pronoun could point to two things.

## Subjects

- Put a named, capitalized Scrum element or a named group in subject position.
- Put an abstract noun in subject position only in a short principle, such as
  "Transparency enables inspection."
- Never put a process noun or an -ing phrase in subject position to carry an argument.

## Conditions

- State a condition as a fronted "If…" in its own sentence. Never use "Where…".
- Otherwise use a modal (may, should, must) or a connective at the start of the sentence:
  However, Regardless, Therefore.
- Give a qualification its own sentence.

## People

- Name people by accountability, in the third person.
- Use "they" for a singular accountability.

## Abstraction

- Turn an abstraction into a named Scrum element in the same sentence or the next.
- State a rule and move on.

## Structure

- Open a section by naming the thing and defining it in one sentence.
- Close a section on a short prescription or consequence.
- Follow a longer statement with a short, flat closer.
- Write a list as a stem ending in a colon, items ending in semicolons, and "and," before
  the last item.
- Use no tables.
- Use American spelling.

## Never

- Write a sentence that exists only to deny something.
- Make the document the subject of a sentence, point to its own sections, or refer to
  another edition.
- Argue for a rule, reason by elimination, or comment on the text's own reasoning.
- Tell the reader to reason.
- Put a metaphor inside a rule.
- Use a contrast slogan: "X, not Y", "not X but Y", "rather than".
- Use a term nobody can observe or check, or a tautology.
- Write a "so that" clause that restates the main clause.
- Coin a term the text does not need.
- Use a term no earlier Scrum Guide uses, unless a new element needs it.

## Change notes

A change note may name the 2020 edition.
