# Inherited guides — how an inherited instance of the Scrum Guide is created

Status: **specification for review. Nothing implemented.**
Date: 2026-11-19
Answers: *"identify how we are going to create inherited instances of the guide so there is a clear extension and adition."*

---

## 1. The question, and a word that means two things

An inherited instance is a guide that does not stand alone: it takes the Scrum
Guide as given and says something further. The site needs a repeatable way to
create one such that a reader can tell, without reading the whole document,
**what it inherits** and **what it adds**.

One obstacle before anything else. "Extension" is in use on this site in two
different senses, and they disagree about the same guide:

| Sense | Means | Test |
|---|---|---|
| **A category** on the site | A guide filed as an Extension rather than an Implementation | What concern does it address? |
| **Something a guide does** | It adds elements to Scrum's framework | Does it add accountabilities, events or artifacts? |

Under the second sense the Nexus Guide is an extension — it says so itself, in
those words. Under the first it is an Implementation, which is the ruling on
record. Both are correct in their own sense, and leaving them tangled is how the
site ends up with a page that says "Nexus extends Scrum" filed under a label
that says it does not.

This document separates them: §4 is what a guide does, §5 is how it is filed.

---

## 2. What the guides already on this site do

This is not a new problem and it does not need a new invention. Two published,
third-party guides on this site are inherited instances, and both state their
inheritance in prose, in the body, near the top. The pattern is theirs, not ours.

**Nexus Guide, 2021.1** — `/nexus-guide/2021.1/`:

> The Nexus framework inherits the purpose and intent of the Scrum framework as
> documented in the Scrum Guide. Scaled Scrum is still Scrum. Nexus does not
> change the core design or ideas of Scrum, or leave out elements, or negate the
> rules of Scrum.

and then, explicitly:

> Nexus extends Scrum in the following ways:
> — **Accountabilities**: The Nexus Integration Team …
> — **Events**: Events are appended to, placed around, or replace regular Scrum events …
> — **Artifacts**: All Scrum Teams use the same, single Product Backlog …

**Kanban Guide for Scrum Teams, 2021.1** — `/kanban-guide-for-scrum-teams/2021.1/`, under a heading called *Relation to the Scrum Guide*:

> This guide does not replace or discount any part of The Scrum Guide. It is
> designed to enhance and expand the practices of Scrum. This guide assumes the
> reader is operating a process using the Scrum framework. Therefore, The Scrum
> Guide applies in its entirety.

Four moves are common to both, and they are the whole answer:

| Move | Nexus | Kanban for Scrum Teams |
|---|---|---|
| Names what it inherits | "inherits the purpose and intent of the Scrum framework" | "The Scrum Guide applies in its entirety" |
| Says what it will not do | "does not change the core design or ideas … or leave out elements, or negate the rules" | "does not replace or discount any part" |
| Says what it adds | A list under Accountabilities, Events, Artifacts | Practices only; adds no framework element |
| Says what it assumes of the reader | A group of three to nine Scrum Teams on one product | "assumes the reader is operating a process using the Scrum framework" |

## 3. Why this is prose and not metadata

The claim has to be **in the document**, not only about it.

- Published PDFs are generated by Pandoc directly from each edition's `index.md`.
  No site template is involved. Anything held only in page metadata is absent
  from the PDF, which is the form most readers cite and archive.
- A guide is read offline, quoted and printed. An inheritance statement that
  exists only as a rendered banner is not part of the work.
- Where text is reused from elsewhere, attribution is required *in the work* by
  the licence it carries, not in a field beside it.

Structured metadata may be worth adding later, to drive a visible list or to
catch a guide that claims something its parent does not say. That is a separate
decision, it is not needed to answer this question, and it must never be the
only place the claim exists.

## 4. How an inherited instance is created

Every inherited guide carries, in its body, before its own definitions, a
section headed **Relation to the Scrum Guide**. Four statements, in this order.
Three are required of every guide; the fourth is required to be answered, even
when the answer is "nothing".

**1. Inheritance.** Name the parent and what is taken from it.

> This guide inherits the purpose and intent of Scrum as stated in the Scrum
> Guide, edition 2026.1. That edition applies in its entirety.

Naming the **edition**, not just "the Scrum Guide", is the one point where this
differs from current practice. Nexus 2021.1 cites `scrumguides.org` without a
version, so a reader cannot tell which Scrum Guide it was written against. On a
site whose whole structure is dated editions, that is a gap worth closing.

**2. Non-derogation.** State the limit, in the parent's terms.

> It does not change, remove or weaken anything the Scrum Guide requires. Where
> this guide is more specific, it constrains; it never relaxes.

This is the rule that makes inheritance mean something. A guide that may quietly
override its parent is not inheriting from it.

**3. Addition — what this guide adds.** The heart of it, and the part that must
be explicit rather than left for the reader to infer. Answer in the parent's own
vocabulary, so the answer is checkable:

- **Accountabilities** this guide adds, or none.
- **Events** this guide adds, replaces or wraps, or none.
- **Artifacts and commitments** this guide adds, or none.
- **Practices** this guide offers, which add no framework element.

"Adds no accountability, event or artifact" is a real and useful answer — it is
the Kanban guide's answer, and stating it is what makes the distinction in §5
visible on the page rather than only in a category label.

**4. Assumption.** What must already be true of the reader for this guide to
apply — that they are running Scrum, how many teams, what context.

### The word to use

Use **adds**, not **extends**, in statement 3. "Extends" is the category name in
§5 and a guide filed as an Implementation should not appear to claim it. Nexus's
own text says "extends", and it is third-party text that stays verbatim — but
guides originated here should say "adds".

## 5. How an inherited guide is filed

Three categories. The test is **the scope of the concern**, not what the guide
adds structurally.

| Category | The concern | Members |
|---|---|---|
| **Foundation** | The definition of Scrum itself | The Scrum Guide |
| **Implementation** | How to do Scrum in some context — more teams, flow, starting out, agentic teams. Sits inside Scrum's purpose of delivering a valuable Increment | Nexus, Kanban Guide for Scrum Teams, Getting Started for Scrum Teams, AI Guide for Scrum Teams |
| **Extension** | Something Scrum does not address at all | Evidence-Based Management, and nothing else at present |

On record, and the reason the structural test was rejected: Nexus adds an
accountability, four events and two artifacts, yet everything it adds is in
service of delivering one Integrated Increment from several teams. That is
Scrum's own purpose, applied to a harder case. It is an implementation.
Evidence-Based Management is about measuring and managing value at an
organisational level — a concern Scrum does not cover, and one that applies in
organisations not using Scrum at all. That is an extension.

Consequences worth stating:

- **An extension makes no inheritance claim.** It is not Scrum, does not claim
  Scrum's intent, and its page must not be framed as though it were. The §4
  statements do not apply to it; what it needs instead is a plain statement of
  what it is for and how it is used alongside Scrum.
- **Extensions are restricted to official ones** — published by Scrum.org — for
  now.
- The Implementation list and "guides that claim this edition's intent" are
  therefore the same set today, and will stop being the same set the moment a
  non-official extension is admitted. They should not be built as one thing.

## 6. What this does not decide

| # | Question |
|---|---|
| 1 | Is Evidence-Based Management hosted here, or listed and linked? If hosted it needs the same per-edition licence review the other guides had, recorded in `docs/licence-review.md`. Its licence has not been checked and must be read from the source, not recalled. |
| 2 | Which licence do the guides originated here carry — 2026, Getting Started, the AI Guide? They are original work, so `docs/licence-review.md` does not cover them. |
| 3 | Do the guides already published get the §4 section retrofitted? Nexus and Kanban for Scrum Teams are verbatim third-party text; adding a section to them would alter that text, so the honest options are an editorial note outside the body or leaving them as they stand. |
| 4 | Does an inherited guide name a single parent edition, or a range it holds for? A guide written against 2026.1 does not automatically hold against 2027.1. |
| 5 | Is there a reverse statement — does the Scrum Guide edition list what claims it, or is inheritance stated only by the inheriting guide? |

## 7. There is no way to do this today

Agreeing §4 and §5 does not create anything. Measured against the installed
platform, v1.1.8:

| Looked for | Found |
|---|---|
| A command to create a new guide | **None.** `New-GuideEdition` requires a `SourceEditionId`, so it can only add an edition to a guide that already exists. A new top-level guide is made entirely by hand |
| A content scaffold | **None.** There are no Hugo archetypes in the site or in the module, so `hugo new` has nothing to fill |
| A way to carry a section into later editions | **`New-GuideEdition`.** It copies an edition's body byte-for-byte, sets `draft`, sets the new version and strips inherited aliases |
| A site-owned validation step | **None.** `build.ps1` exposes a fixed set of stages and `Invoke-GuideSiteBuild` has no hook a site can add a check to |

So the two guides to be created next — Getting Started for Scrum Teams and the
AI Guide for Scrum Teams — are hand-made directories today, and nothing would
notice if the §4 section were missing, worded differently in each, or dropped
from a later edition.

## 8. Options

Two separate choices. **A** is where the words come from when a guide is
created. **B** is how we know they are still there and still right.

### A — Creating it

| | Option | Costs | Gets you | Doesn't |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A1** | By hand from §4 each time | Nothing | Immediate start | Three guides, three wordings; nothing to review a guide *against* |
| **A2** | A normative template in the repo — one file holding the §4 section with the parent edition left blank, copied when a guide is created | One file | One agreed wording, reviewable as text, and a thing a reviewer can diff a guide against | Enforce anything |
| **A3** | A Hugo archetype | One file, plus adopting a scaffolding path the platform does not use | `hugo new` support | Help at all with a new *guide* bundle, which is the actual gap |
| **A4** | Seed later editions with `New-GuideEdition` | Nothing — it exists | Every subsequent edition of a guide inherits the section for free, byte-for-byte | Help with the first edition of a new guide |
| **A5** | Propose a whole-guide scaffold to OpenGuidePlatform | Upstream work, another release cycle | Fixes it for every site, properly | Help now |

A2 and A4 are complementary, not alternatives: A2 solves the first edition, A4
solves every edition after it.

### B — Keeping it honest

| | Option | Costs | Gets you | Doesn't |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **B1** | Nothing | Nothing | — | Catch an omission |
| **B2** | A review checklist in `docs/content-management.md` | A few lines | A named step before a guide is published, and a reason to say no | Catch anything nobody reviews |
| **B3** | A site-owned script that checks every edition has the section and its four statements | A script, run by hand or in CI; it cannot live in `build.ps1` because there is no hook | Actual enforcement | Judge whether the words are *true* — only that they are present |
| **B4** | Propose a content-convention check upstream | Upstream work | Enforcement for every site | Help now |
| **B5** | Mirror the prose in front matter and check it at build time | Six template files and a specification rewrite — this was built, reverted in `1f6c456`, and is on record as unwanted | A machine-checkable claim and a rendered list | Appear in the PDF, which is where the claim has to be |

B3 and B5 both check presence, not truth. Whether a guide really adds no
accountability is a judgement, and no check will make it.

### Recommended

**A2 + A4, and B2.**

A template file and a checklist, and let `New-GuideEdition` carry the section
forward. That is two small files and no tooling, it makes the wording consistent
across the three guides being written now, and it gives a reviewer something to
check against rather than an opinion.

Not B3 yet. A script that enforces a convention across three guides costs more
than reading three guides. Revisit when there are ten, or the first time a guide
ships without the section.

A5 is worth raising upstream regardless of what is chosen here, because "there
is no way to create a new guide" is a platform gap and not ours alone.

### If A2 is chosen

The template is one file, `docs/templates/relation-to-the-scrum-guide.md`, holding
the §4 section with the parent edition and the four answers left blank. It is
normative: a guide departs from its wording deliberately, not by accident. 2026.1,
Getting Started and the AI Guide each carry it from their first draft, because
retrofitting it afterwards is how it ends up inconsistent.
